Adaptive perturbation mapping identifies a context-dependent immune signaling circuit

Abstract

Cell-state-specific responses to perturbation remain difficult to predict. We combined perturbation screening with single-cell sequencing to identify a signaling circuit that changes across inflammatory contexts. The model improved prediction of response compared with a baseline classifier.

Introduction

Immune signaling is shaped by the microenvironment, but most screening studies average away cell-state differences. We asked whether a context-aware model could reveal interpretable regulatory relationships.

Methods

We profiled three donor-derived cultures under control and inflammatory conditions. Guide abundance, viability, and transcriptional state were measured at 24 and 72 hours. Statistical comparisons used donor-aware mixed models.

Results

The context-aware model significantly improved response prediction relative to the baseline model (Fig. 1b). Perturbation of regulator X increased pathway activation only in inflammatory cultures (Fig. 2a). Inhibition of mediator Y reduced the phenotype and supported a context-dependent mechanism (Fig. 3c).

Discussion

These results suggest that regulatory effects can be conditional on inflammatory state. The limited donor number and in-vitro model constrain generalization.
